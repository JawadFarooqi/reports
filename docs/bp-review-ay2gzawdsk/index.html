    Is there a market for BotPool Create?

    Yes: many small businesses still have no website, most already use AI, and interest in AI website builders has grown about four times in the last two years. But the market is crowded, and BotPool is not yet set up to win it.

            Conclusion

            There is a real market for BotPool Create. One in five small businesses still has no website, most already use AI, and the owners who need a site care more about a finished result than about building apps. BotPool Create is the only builder reviewed with an integrated expert handover, and it already leads on clear pricing, credit costs shown up front, and the best score of the three on Google's phone speed test.

            What holds it back is the route in. A visitor who wants a website starts on a freelancer homepage, fills in a long sign-up form, confirms their email, logs in again, lands in the marketplace and has to type their idea a second time: 18 actions, against about 5 on Lovable and Base44. The integrated expert handover, its main differentiator, only appears after a site is built, while Wix and Squarespace already promote expert help openly, and the Create page doesn't show up in small-business searches.

            Fix the route first, then make the expert visible and get found:

            1. Give website visitors a way in from botpool.ai's first screen
            2. Keep what visitors type through sign-up, make sign-up one click, and land new sign-ups in the builder
            3. Show the expert option before the first site and rank for small-business searches

            Everything else in the priority list refines a route that already works.
